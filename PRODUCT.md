## Platform

web

## Stack

Next.js 16 App Router, React 19, TypeScript, Vercel-ready. Chosen by the user to
match the two sites already shipped for businesses of this kind
(`phoenix-detailing`, `ironclad`), so the sourcing discipline in `content/site.ts`
carries over unchanged. The visual world is deliberately not carried over.

## Users

Car owners in and around Peterborough who want their car cleaned properly and
are choosing between MKS and the hand car wash down the road. Most arrive on a
phone, from a Google listing or an Instagram post, already half-decided, and
want two things fast: proof the work is good, and a way to ask a price.

A second, smaller audience matters more than its size: the local modified-car
scene. The work on show is a bagged widebody 370z and an Audi S3 on aftermarket
wheels, cars whose owners are specific about who touches their paint. They are
the reason a generic valet-service tone would cost this business customers.

## Product Purpose

MKS Detailing is a real valeting and detailing business operating from 1 First
Drove, Peterborough. The site exists so a stranger can check the business out
and message it. Success is a WhatsApp message, not a session length.

## Positioning

A 5.0 rating across 42 Google reviews, with no rating below it pulling the
average down, alongside reviews that name price as a reason to come. Careful
work at a price people call unbeatable is the claim, and it is the customers'
claim, not the business's.

The photographic evidence supports a narrower one the copy may lean on: the
enthusiast cars in Peterborough are brought here.

## Operating Context

Work happens outdoors on a concrete forecourt, cars photographed in daylight
against a row of houses with solar panels, other customer cars parked along the
back. This is a working yard, not a white studio, and the photography must not
be dressed up into one.

Enquiries arrive by WhatsApp, phone, email or Instagram DM. The business's own
contact card leads with WhatsApp, so the site does too.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Services are confirmed only as far as the business's own captions prove them:
  a maintenance clean, an exterior detail, and interior work using an air
  compressor for carpets and trims. The full service list has not been supplied.
- No prices of any kind have been supplied. The site publishes none, and routes
  price questions to WhatsApp.
- Opening hours are known only as "closes 6 pm" from the Google listing. The
  full weekly hours have not been supplied and are not to be guessed.
- No domain has been agreed. The site publishes no canonical URL, no Open Graph
  URL and no `url` in its structured data until one exists.
- The name appears two ways in source material: "Mks Detailing" on the Google
  listing, "MK'S DETAILING" on the logo badge. The logo spelling is the
  business's own and wins for display.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: MK'S DETAILING. Handle `@mksdetailingg` on both Instagram and TikTok.
- Existing mark: a black disc carrying a white front-elevation sports car
  silhouette above a condensed uppercase "MK'S DETAILING" wordmark. Only a
  low-resolution copy exists, taken from the Instagram avatar.
- The site must carry a before/after comparison of the Audi S3, driven by a
  slider the visitor drags. Requested explicitly by the user and binding.

## Product Principles

1. Nothing on the page may be invented. Every claim traces to the business's own
   published material or to a customer's own words, noted inline at its source.
2. A missing fact stays a visible empty slot with the UI hiding what depends on
   it. It never gets a plausible-looking placeholder.
3. The rating is the strongest asset and it is real, so it is shown as a number
   and stands behind structured data. It is also the only number on the page.
4. The photographs are the proof. Four exist, they are phone shots from a
   working yard, and the design has to be built for that rather than around it.
5. Every road leads to a message. A visitor who is convinced should never have
   to look for how to ask.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Phone-first, outdoors, one-handed. The before/after slider is the one piece of
real interaction on the page, so it must be operable by keyboard and expose its
position to assistive technology, not only respond to a drag.
